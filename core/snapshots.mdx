A snapshot is a saved view of your portfolio at a specific time. It is the main unit of history in Retina.

## What a snapshot contains

A snapshot can include:

- Positions from EVM and Solana wallets
- Hyperliquid positions associated with tracked addresses
- Polymarket balances and positions
- Manual positions and metals
- Prices, APYs, maturities, liquidity, and position classifications
- The tracked source configuration used for that capture

Older snapshots keep the values recorded at capture time. They do not change when prices or provider data change later.

## Create a snapshot

Select **+ Snapshot** from the app header.

- **Add snapshot now** captures the sources you already track. Retina's servers run the capture, with or without automatic updates, so you can keep using the app, close the tab, or lock your phone. A notice at the bottom of the app shows how many sources Retina has read and an estimate of the time left. When the snapshot is saved, the notice says **Your new snapshot is ready**; select **Show it** to load the new snapshot. If you closed the tab, the app opens on the new snapshot the next time you visit.
- **Change what you track** opens setup, where you add, remove, or relabel sources first. When you select **Create snapshot**, Retina saves your changes to your account, starts the capture on its servers and opens the dashboard, where the same notice shows the progress.

Some captures run in your browser instead: the first snapshot of a new portfolio, and any snapshot of a portfolio with pasted addresses, because only your browser holds the pasted list. Keep the page open until that snapshot is saved.

Retina checks snapshot access and account limits before starting. Settings, the **+ Snapshot** choice, setup, the capture reservation, and the final save all use the same current allowance from your account. The app shows the next available capture time when a cooldown applies.

## Daily limits and retries

Focus includes 1 automatic and 1 manual snapshot allowance each day. Range and Horizon include 2 automatic and 1 manual allowance.

An automatic slot can become available for manual use when no automatic run remains due that day. Settings includes that slot in the manual allowance for the rest of the day, and the snapshot flow uses the same effective total. Successful captures are kept at least 1 hour apart.

When a wallet cannot be read, Retina shows **No snapshot saved** and spends no capture slot. Select **Try again** after a few minutes. If the account's data allowance is used, it resets at midnight UTC.

Pendle positions have one exception. When Pendle cannot be reached, Retina can keep the value Pendle last gave a position for up to 48 hours from when Pendle priced it, as long as nothing moved that position since: no deposit, withdrawal, transfer or stake of its LP, PT or YT. Other positions in the wallet are read as usual. The snapshot is saved, and the next snapshot Pendle answers brings the value up to date. Earned APY leaves those days out, so a reused value never counts as earnings. A small Pendle position that no other source can price, such as a PT after its maturity date, keeps the value per token Pendle last gave it for as long as Pendle cannot be reached. A small Pendle position that no longer appears in your wallet data, with no transaction to explain the change, keeps its last value for up to 48 hours and is left out of snapshots after that. Both rules apply only while those small positions together are worth at most $25 and at most 0.01% of your portfolio. A few dollars of an expired or vanished position can no longer stop a snapshot of everything else.

## Review before saving

Retina checks whether required sources returned complete results and whether large changes can be explained. A change that a transaction Retina captured accounts for, such as a withdrawal from a protocol, is explained and does not pause the snapshot.

For a capture that runs in your browser, you may be asked to:

- Refresh an affected wallet
- Exclude an incorrect asset and recalculate
- Continue when you recognize the result as correct

A snapshot keeps the time your wallets were read, even when you continue some time later. If a newer snapshot was saved while it waited, create a new snapshot instead.

Captures that Retina's servers run use the same principle. These are automatic updates and **Add snapshot now**. A blocking finding becomes an unsaved snapshot review instead of entering history. An advisory finding can be saved with a visible flag. Open **Updates** from the sidebar, or from **More** on a phone, to review either state.

A server review compares the previous and proposed totals and shows the balances that caused the pause. For each balance, choose **Use this value** or **Check again**. You can confirm a known deposit while rechecking a different protocol or wallet balance. Retina targets only the wallets connected to the selected balance and keeps the complete snapshot unsaved until the remaining checks pass.

## Select and manage snapshots

Use the snapshot selector at the top of the sidebar to change the active snapshot. Most app views follow that selection. On a wallet, protocol or token page, every chart, calendar and activity list ends at the selected snapshot. The History view always shows the full timeline.

From snapshot management you can:

- Open a snapshot
- Rename it
- Delete it
- Export portfolio data
- Compare it with earlier snapshots in History

Deleting a synced snapshot also updates the account copy after the deletion is confirmed. Deletion does not restore a daily capture allowance that was already used.

## Live data inside snapshot views

Most totals come from the active snapshot. Some surfaces deliberately fetch newer public information, especially current Polymarket data and yield information. Retina labels these states so you can tell saved values from refreshed values.
