Running entirely in the browser has real downsides. The right way to read this section is: here are the constraints, here is how Retina works around them, here is what is still genuinely worse.

## One device at a time

Your wallets and snapshots live in browser storage on the device and browser you added them on. If you open Retina on a different machine, the wallet list is empty.

The current workaround is to re-add wallets on the new device. The wallet list is short for most operators; this is annoying but not catastrophic.

Multi-device sync is on the [Roadmap](/roadmap).

## Clearing browser data wipes Retina

Clearing site data for `useretina.xyz` removes your wallets, labels, custom positions, and snapshots. So does using a browser's incognito mode that does not persist storage.

<Warning>
  If you intend to keep history, do not clear site data and do not switch browsers without exporting first. {/* TODO: confirm export flow exists in the UI */}
</Warning>

## You re-enter custom positions

Manual positions only exist where you entered them. Treat custom positions as device-local data.

## Refresh cost is on you

Every refresh hits the upstream APIs from your browser. Rate limits affect you directly. The app is designed to be conservative with calls, but it cannot pre-warm a cache the way a hosted product can.

## What is genuinely worse

- No multi-device sync today.
- No background snapshotting when the tab is closed.
- No team-shared portfolios.

If those are deal-breakers, Retina is not the right tool yet. See [Roadmap toward sync](/privacy/roadmap-toward-sync) for what changes when sync ships.
